# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Keeptrail is for designers, independent builders, researchers, students, and developers who save useful videos, links, and images across the web and later struggle to retrieve a specific idea or resource. The primary context is focused desktop work on a MacBook Air: a user remembers a phrase, visual detail, or website but not the original platform or title. Coding agents are a secondary audience and may retrieve read-only passages from the local library.

## Product Purpose

Keeptrail turns saved sources into a local discovery library of searchable notes, evidence, captures, topics, tags, and meaningful connections. Its primary job is to help a user find the website or idea they remember seeing. Its secondary job is to let them explore shared topics and sources without losing the evidence behind each connection. Success means a user can import one source, identify a useful extracted detail, return to its evidence, correct the metadata, and find it again later.

## Brand Personality

Curious, grounded, clear. The interface should feel like a quiet personal reference desk: calm enough for focused retrieval, structured enough to trust, and open enough to invite exploration. Use plain international English, familiar verbs, and source-aware language. Keep the emotional promise small and concrete: “Find your way back.”

## Anti-references

Do not look like a generic AI dashboard, a productivity guilt machine, or a decorative graph demo. Avoid giant marketing headings, gradients, glow, glass blur, continuous animated nodes, fake counters, avatars without function, emoji navigation, railway jargon as the primary interface vocabulary, and unsupported claims about speed, privacy, or free AI. Do not use color alone to communicate state or relationship. Do not hide evidence or overwrite the user's manual notes and confirmations.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with retrieval: search and source evidence are the shortest path to value.
2. Keep the trail intact: every useful claim should point back to a source, timestamp, capture, or note.
3. Make exploration legible: routes and connections describe real shared topics or explicit mentions, while suggested similarity is labeled separately.
4. Preserve ownership: local storage, editable metadata, export, deletion controls, and honest provider boundaries are part of the product experience.
5. Prefer calm, familiar controls over novelty: density is useful when hierarchy, focus, and state feedback remain clear.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for the web interface. Support keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic landmarks, accessible names, reduced motion, readable contrast, and a list alternative for the map. Never rely on color alone. Preserve browser zoom, use effective touch targets of at least 44px, keep long text readable, and restore focus after drawers and route changes. Error states must explain what happened and what the user can do next.
